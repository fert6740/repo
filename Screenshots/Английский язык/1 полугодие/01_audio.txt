Hello, everyone. My name is Daniel Stevenson, and I'm going to tell you about my recent trip to the North Pole. You all probably expect to hear a story about survival at extremely low temperatures, but fortunately, that's not what happened to me. The temperature in winter there did go as low as negative 40 degrees, but I visited the North Pole in summer, so it was about zero, and I found it quite comfortable. Still, I had to take warm and waterproof clothes with me, and they took most of the space in my luggage. But the thing I found most useful was my sunglasses. White snow everywhere reflects sunlight, and it's very harmful for your eyes to look at nature in all its glory. For the same reason, wearing sunscreen on your face is highly recommended.

Though inaccessible for most of the year, it is possible to travel to the North Pole by icebreaker in June and July when the ice is thinner. I chose to sail there from Murmansk. A huge icebreaker ship is truly amazing. It's one of the biggest things I've seen. However, it offers pretty small cabins and very little space for luggage. At first, I had no idea where to put all my stuff, but then I found a large cupboard under the bed there. That was unexpected.

The atmosphere on board the ship was great. There were tourists from different countries and the crew did everything to make our journey as comfortable as possible. Our tour guide came from Norway and he organized all our events and activities. I liked that he was really friendly and his stories were informative. As to the weather, well, the ocean is usually stormy in winter, but in July it was calm and significantly less windy. That was very good for me because I was worried about getting seasick. Luckily, I was okay, as well as most of the other passengers.

Did I like it? Yes, absolutely. I was blown away by the spectacle of the ship pushing through the ice. While there is not much wildlife at the pole itself, we were lucky to see polar bears, seals, walrus, whales, and plenty of birds along the way. And the most amazing moment of the expedition happened when we got to the geographic North Pole, a place so few people have been to. The atmosphere there was very special, and everyone got very excited. We had a lot of fun running through all the time zones as quickly as possible.

Of course, the North Pole doesn't have many people living there, and the only people we saw apart from tourists were a few of the scientists at a research station. They welcomed us and gave us tea and biscuits. I guess their life in the North is really tough and lonely, so they were genuinely happy to see us.

I'm often asked if I feel guilty about disturbing such an untouched region as the North Pole. I guess yes and no. There are only around five trips per year for 640 passengers. Ships aren't allowed to dump rubbish or to go where they like, and they have to take scientists to lead the excursions. There are rules, of course. Only small parties are permitted to land in one area at a time, and you've got to keep quiet and not bother the wildlife. So, I feel that well-run trips like this one do more good than harm. I also felt completely changed by the experience. It felt like going to another world.
